This little heart turns spare yarn into a thoughtful finishing touch. Try one on a gift tag or make several for a garland. This is demo editorial content awaiting a human maker's stitch and shape review. Instructions use US crochet terms.

## At a glance
- Skill level: beginner who has practiced taller stitches
- Construction: one round worked into a magic ring
- Abbreviations: ch = chain, dc = double crochet, tr = treble crochet, sl st = slip stitch

## Materials
- A small amount of cotton yarn in a color you love
- Optional matching thread for attaching the heart

## Tools
- 3.5 mm crochet hook
- Tapestry needle
- Scissors

## Instructions
### 1. Start a loose ring
Make a magic ring and leave a long tail. Keep the ring open while you work all of the stitches into it.

### 2. Shape the first lobe
Ch 3. Work 3 tr, then 3 dc into the ring. These taller stitches make the rounded top of the heart.

### 3. Make the point and second lobe
Ch 1, work 1 tr into the ring for the bottom point, then ch 1. Work 3 dc and 3 tr into the ring, mirroring the first side.

### 4. Close and finish
Ch 3 and make a sl st into the ring. Pull the ring tail gently until the center closes. Fasten off, weave in the ends, and shape the two lobes with your fingers.

## Tips
- The center treble stitch forms the point; keep it a little loose so it can sit below the two sides.
- If the heart curls, lightly dampen it and pin it flat while drying.
- Leave a longer tail if you plan to sew it onto fabric.

## Common mistakes
- Pulling the ring closed too early makes later stitches hard to fit. Wait until the whole heart is complete.
- Uneven tension between the two sets of tall stitches can make one lobe larger. Aim for the same relaxed tension on both sides.

## FAQ
### Can I make a larger heart?
Use thicker yarn and a hook sized for it. The shape will grow, but the stitch sequence can stay the same.

### Can I hang it as an ornament?
Yes. Attach a short loop through the center top after finishing.
