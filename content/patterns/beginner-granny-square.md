A granny square is a satisfying first motif: one small center grows into a tidy square you can use for a blanket or a bag. This is demo editorial content awaiting a human stitch-count and gauge review. Instructions use US crochet terms.

## At a glance
- Skill level: beginner
- Construction: worked in joined rounds
- Abbreviations: ch = chain, dc = double crochet, sl st = slip stitch

## Variations
- Use a single color for all three rounds to focus on stitch placement before trying color changes.
- Try a thicker yarn and its recommended hook for a larger motif; check the finished size before joining squares.

## Materials
- Three small amounts of medium-weight cotton yarn in different colors
- A few extra centimeters of each color for weaving in ends

## Tools
- 4 mm crochet hook, or the size recommended for your yarn
- Tapestry needle
- Scissors

## Instructions
### 1. Make a center ring
Ch 4 and join the first chain with a sl st to make a small ring.

### 2. Work round one
Ch 3; this counts as the first dc. Work 2 dc into the ring, then ch 2. Work three more groups of 3 dc into the ring, placing ch 2 between groups. After the fourth group, ch 2 and join to the top of the starting ch 3. You should have four clusters and four corner spaces.

### 3. Work round two
Join a new color in any corner space. In that space, work ch 3, 2 dc, ch 2, 3 dc. Ch 1. In each of the other three corner spaces, work 3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc, followed by ch 1. Join to the top of the starting ch 3.

### 4. Work round three
Join your third color in a corner space. Work a corner as in round two: 3 dc, ch 2, 3 dc. Ch 1, then work 3 dc into the side ch-1 space. Ch 1 and repeat around, working the same corner group in each corner space and one 3-dc group in each side space. Join and fasten off.

### 5. Finish
Weave in all loose ends. Gently block the square so its corners sit evenly.

## Tips
- The ch-2 spaces are corners; the ch-1 spaces sit along the sides.
- Turn the square between rounds if it begins to twist.
- Make several squares with the same hook and yarn before joining them into a larger item.

## Common mistakes
- Putting a side cluster into a corner space changes the shape. Pause and identify all four ch-2 corners before each round.
- Pulling the starting chain too tightly can make the center pucker. Keep the ring loose enough to work into.

## FAQ
### Can I keep adding rounds?
Yes. Each new round adds another side cluster between the corner groups.

### Does the square need three colors?
No. Use one color throughout if you prefer fewer ends to weave in.
