This tote uses only straightforward rows, so you can focus on an even fabric and careful assembly. It is a longer beginner project that feels rewarding when the pieces come together. ## At a glance
- Skill level: adventurous beginner
- Construction: two flat panels plus two separate straps
- Approximate finished size: 30 cm wide by 32 cm tall, depending on gauge
- Abbreviations: ch = chain, sc = single crochet, sl st = slip stitch

## Materials
- Approximately 400 m of sturdy medium-weight cotton yarn
- Optional cotton fabric lining if you plan to carry small objects

## Tools
- 4 mm crochet hook, or a size that makes a firm fabric
- Tapestry needle
- Stitch markers
- Scissors

## Instructions
### 1. Make the first panel
Ch 41. Starting in the second chain from the hook, work 40 sc. Ch 1 and turn. Work 1 sc in each stitch for 39 more rows, or until the panel is about 32 cm tall. Keep 40 stitches in every row.

### 2. Make a matching panel
Repeat the first panel with the same yarn and hook. Check that both pieces have the same number of rows and lie flat. Add rows to the shorter panel if needed.

### 3. Join the sides and bottom
Place the panels together with their outer faces touching. Use matching yarn to seam the left side, bottom, and right side. Leave the top open, then turn the bag right side out.

### 4. Crochet two straps
For each strap, ch 61. Starting in the second chain from the hook, work 60 sc. Ch 1 and turn; repeat for three more rows, keeping 60 stitches per row. Fasten off with long sewing tails.

### 5. Attach and reinforce
Pin each strap end about 8 cm in from a side seam on the inside top edge. Sew the ends down through several rows of the panel, then sew a small square or cross of stitches over each join. Repeat for the second strap and weave in all tails.

## Tips
- Make a small swatch first: tighter single crochet gives the bag more structure.
- Use stitch markers at the first and last stitch of each panel row to keep the edges straight.
- Test the bag with a light load before carrying anything heavy.

## Common mistakes
- Panels with different row counts will twist the finished bag. Compare them before seaming.
- Narrow or loosely sewn strap joins can stretch. Reinforce each join and use a lining if the bag will hold small or heavy items.

## FAQ
### Can I make the tote wider?
Add more starting chains to both panels and keep the same stitch count in every row.

### Is the bag suitable for groceries?
This draft is designed for light everyday items. A human maker should test the finished bag and reinforced straps before using it for heavier loads.
