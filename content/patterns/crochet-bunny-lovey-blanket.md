## About This Pattern

This free crochet pattern creates a bunny lovey blanket — a sweet security blanket topped with a soft amigurumi bunny head, long floppy ears, and two little arms that seem to hug the blanket beneath them. Worked in white with light pink and dark pink accents, it is the kind of project that makes everyone at the baby shower gasp. It is perfect for new parents, first birthdays, or any little one who needs a snuggly companion that is equal parts toy and blanket

You will work the bunny's head in continuous rounds, shape the 14-round ears and 17-round arms in pairs, and crochet the blanket itself across 17 gently increasing rounds until it reaches about 20 inches at its widest point. The techniques are all classics — single crochet, evenly spaced increases, simple color changes, and a bit of stuffing — so a confident beginner can absolutely manage it. Most crocheters finish the pieces in about 4 to 6 hours, plus a little quiet time for the 4 assembly steps at the end

The finished lovey measures roughly 13½ inches when folded (not counting those glorious ears) and about 20 inches across the blanket at its widest point. Worsted weight yarn gives the head a plush, squeezable feel and the blanket a soft drape that tiny hands can grip and drag everywhere. The dark brown embroidered eyes keep everything soft and baby-friendly, while the pink details make it photograph beautifully. Wrap it up with a handwritten care note and you have a handmade present nobody will ever forget

## Instructions

### Lovey Blanket

#### Round 1

**Pattern:** With light pink, ch 4, join to form ring, ch 3 (counts as 1st dc here and throughout), 11 dc in ring, join w/sl st to top of beginning ch 3 (12 dc)

#### Round 2

**Pattern:** Ch 3, 1 dc in same st, 2 dc in ea st around. Join w/top of ch 3. (24 dc)

#### Round 3

**Pattern:** Ch 3, *skip next st (1 dc, ch 2, 1 dc) in next st, repeat from * around. End row with a dc in base of ch 3, ch 2, join with sl st to top of ch 3.

#### Round 4

**Pattern:** Sl st into ch 2 sp, ch 3 (1 dc, ch 2, 2 dc) in ch 2 sp, *(2 dc, ch 2, 2dc) in next ch 2 sp, repeat from * around. Join. Fasten off light pink.

#### Round 5

**Pattern:** Attach dark pink to next ch 2 sp. *(2sc, ch 2, 2 sc) in ch 2 sp, 1 sc in next st, skip next 2 st, 1 sc in next st, repeat from * around, end w/sc. Join. Fasten off dark pink.

#### Round 6

**Pattern:** With white, join w/sl st in next ch 2 sp, ch 3 (1dc, ch 2, 2 dc) in ch 2 sp, 1dc in next 2 st, skip 2 st, 1 dc in next 2 st, *(2dc, ch 2, 2 dc) in ch 2 sp, 1dc in next 2 st, skip 2 st, 1 dc in next 2 st, repeat from * around. End w/2 dc. Join.

#### Round 7

**Pattern:** Ch 3, dc in next dc, *(1 dc, ch 2, 1 dc) in ch 2 sp, dc in next 3 st, skip 2 st, dc in next 3 st, repeat from *, end with dc in last st and join.

#### Round 8

**Pattern:** Ch 3, dc in next 2 dc, *(2 dc, ch 2, 2dc) in 2 sp, dc in next 3 st, skip 2 st, dc in next 3 st. Repeat from * around. Join w/top of ch 3. Fasten off white

#### Round 9

**Pattern:** With dark pink, join w/sl st in next ch 2 sp, ch 1, *(2sc, ch 2, 2 sc) in ch 2 sp, sc in next 4 st, skip 2 st, sc in next 4 st, repeat from * around. Join. Fasten off dark pink

#### Round 10

**Pattern:** With light pink, join w/sl st in st before a ch 2 sp, ch 3 (counts as dc), *(1dc, ch 2, 1 dc) in ch 2 sp, 1dc in next 5 st, skip 2 st, 1 dc in next 5 st, repeat from * around. End by dc in last 4 st, join.

#### Round 11

**Pattern:** Ch 3, dc in next dc, *(2 dc, ch 2, 2dc) in ch 2 sp, dc in next 5 st, skip 2 dc, dc in next 5 st, repeat from * around and end by dc in last 3 st & join.

#### Round 12

**Pattern:** Ch 3, dc in next dc 3 st, *(2 dc, ch 2, 2dc) in ch 2 sp, dc in next 6 st, skip 2 st, dc in next 6 st, repeat from * around and end by dc in last 2 st & join. Fasten off light pink.

#### Round 13

**Pattern:** Join dark pink in ch 2 sp, ch1, *(sc, ch 2, sc) in ch 2 sp, sc in next 7 st, skip 2 st, sc in next 7 st, repeat from * around. Join. Fasten off dark pink.

#### Round 14

**Pattern:** Join white in sc before a ch 2 sp, ch 3, *(2 dc, ch 2, 2dc) in ch 2 sp, dc in next 7 st, skip 2 sc, dc in next 7 st, repeat from * around. End row by dc in last 6 st & join.

#### Round 15

**Pattern:** Ch 3, dc in next 2 dc, *(2 dc, ch 2, 2dc) in ch 2 sp, dc in next 8 st, skip 2 st, dc in next 8 st, repeat from * around. End row by dc in last 5 st & join.

#### Round 16

**Pattern:** Ch 3, dc in next 4 dc, *(dc, ch 2, dc) in ch 2 sp, dc in next 9 st, skip 2 st, dc in next 9 st, repeat from * around. End row by dc in last 5 st & join. Fasten off white.

#### Round 17

**Pattern:** Join dark pink in ch 2 sp, ch1, *(2sc, ch 2, 2sc) in ch 2 sp, sc in next 9 st, skip 2 st, sc in next 9 st, repeat from * around. Join. Fasten off and weave in ends

### Crochet Bunny Head

#### Round 1

**Pattern:** With white, ch 2, 6 sc in 2nd chain from hook. (6 st) Work in rounds.

#### Round 2

**Pattern:** 2 sc in ea st (12 st)

#### Round 3

**Pattern:** *sc in next st, 2 sc in next. Repeat from * around. (18 st)

#### Round 4

**Pattern:** *sc in next 2 st, 2 sc in next. Repeat from * around. (24 st)

#### Round 5

**Pattern:** *sc in next 3 st, 2 sc in next. Repeat from * around. (30 st)

#### Round 6

**Pattern:** *sc in next 4 st, 2 sc in next. Repeat from * around. (36 st)

#### Round 7-14

**Pattern:** sc around. (36 st)

#### Round 15

**Pattern:** *sc in next 4 st, sc next 2 st together. Repeat from * around (30)

#### Round 16

**Pattern:** *sc in next 3 st, sc next 2 st together. Repeat from * around (24)

#### Round 17

**Pattern:** *sc in next 2 st, sc next 2 st together. Repeat from * around (18) Stuff.

#### Round 18

**Pattern:** *sc in next st, sc next 2 st together. Repeat from * around (12)

#### Round 19

**Pattern:** *sc next 2 st together. Repeat from * around (6 st). Fasten off, leaving a long strand for finishing. Weave through last 6 stitches, pull tight, weaving in ends or knotting of to secure.

**Pattern:** sc around (9 st). Fasten off, leaving a long strand for sewing.

### Arms (make 2)

#### Round 1

**Pattern:** With white, ch 2, 5 sc in 2nd ch. (5 st) Work in rounds

#### Round 2

**Pattern:** 2 sc in ea st (10 st)

#### Round 3

**Pattern:** *sc in next st, 2 sc in next st. Repeat from * around (15 st)

#### Round 4-6

**Pattern:** sc around (15 st)

#### Round 7

**Pattern:** *sc in next st, sc next 2 st together. Repeat from * around. (10 st).

#### Round 8-17

**Pattern:** sc around (10 st). Sl st to next sc and fasten off leaving a long strand for finishing. Stuff firmly, leaving the top ½” unstuffed.

## Important Notes
- Use the recommended US H8/5.00 mm hook, but if your single crochet fabric looks gappy, go down to a G/4.0 mm for the head, ears, and arms only — a tighter fabric keeps stuffing hidden while the blanket can stay soft and drapey at the larger gauge
- Stuff the head gradually as the closing rounds shrink the opening. Waiting until the very end means fighting polyfil through a tiny gap, and you will almost always under-fill the top of the head where the ears attach
- Leave 12-inch tails when you fasten off the ears and arms. All 4 assembly steps go faster and look cleaner when you sew each piece on with its own tail instead of joining new yarn, and you will have fewer ends to weave in
- If this lovey is for a baby, embroider the eyes with the dark brown yarn exactly as the pattern intends rather than swapping in plastic safety eyes, and knot every tail on the inside of the head. Give each ear and arm a firm tug test after assembly
- Keep the white, light pink, and dark pink skeins in separate zip bags while you work. The pattern's color changes are simple, but worsted weight strands love to tangle, and clean changes at the end of a round hide much better than mid-round swaps
- Choose a machine-washable worsted acrylic since a loved lovey will need frequent baths. About 150 yards of each main color covers the whole project, so one standard skein per color leaves comfortable margin for the ears and arms
- This pattern is meant to be a springboard for your own lovey creations. Swap out colors, change the ears and embroidered features to transform the animal, or pair the bunny head with a different blanket style worked in the round — just stop when the lovey is about 18-22 inches across at its widest point, or whatever size looks good to you

## Common Mistakes

### Losing your place in the continuous rounds of the bunny head

Amigurumi rounds spiral without joins, so it is very easy to drift a stitch or two per round and end up with a lopsided head. Place a stitch marker in the first stitch of every round and move it up as you go, and count your stitches at the end of each increase round before moving on

### Leaving the starting ring loose before stuffing

A loose starting round will slowly reopen once you pack polyfil behind it, leaving a hole at the top of the head where stuffing peeks out. Pull the starting tail firmly closed before working past round 3, then weave the tail through the opening stitches a second time and knot it on the inside. Give the magic ring that starts each ear the same firm tug

### Overstuffing the head so the stitches stretch and gape

Worsted weight single crochet fabric will show white polyfil through the gaps if you cram it full. Stuff firmly at the base so the head sits upright on the blanket, but stop the moment the fabric starts to strain. Roll the head between your palms to distribute the stuffing evenly instead of adding more

### The 17-round blanket ruffling or

Ruffling means extra stitches crept in; cupping means you skipped increases. Keep the increase spacing consistent from round to round, count after every round, and lay the blanket flat on a table every few rounds. It should relax flat on its own well before it reaches the full 20-inch width

### Mismatched pairs on the ears and arms

The ears take 14 rounds each and the arms 17, and tension drift between the first and second piece is the number one reason pairs come out different sizes. Make each pair back-to-back in the same sitting, with the same hook and mood, and hold them together to compare before fastening off the second one

### Embroidering the dark brown eyes unevenly

Freehand eye placement almost always ends up crooked. Before threading your tapestry needle, mark both eye positions with locking stitch markers or pins on the same round of the head, step back and look at the face straight on, and only then embroider. Wrap the yarn the same number of passes for each eye so they match in size

## FAQ

### Is this bunny lovey safe for a newborn?

The pattern is baby-friendly by design — the eyes are embroidered with dark brown yarn instead of plastic parts, and there are no buttons or beads. Sew the ears, arms, and head on very securely and knot ends inside the head. As with any blanket, supervised snuggles only for the youngest babies

### How much yarn do I really need?

Plan on about 150 yards each of worsted weight in white, light pink, and dark pink, plus a single yard of dark brown for the eyes. That means one standard skein per main color is plenty, with leftovers for a second set of ears if you want to experiment

### Do I stuff the blanket part of the lovey?

No — only the bunny head gets polyfil stuffing, packed firmly at the base so it sits up on the blanket. The arms are stuffed firmly with the top half inch left unstuffed for sewing, and the 17-round blanket stays flat and drapey so it folds to about 13½ inches

### Can I make the blanket bigger for a toddler?

Yes. Either continue the blanket's increase pattern for 2–3 rounds beyond round 17, or work the whole pattern in bulky yarn with a J/6.0 mm hook for a roughly 25 percent larger lovey. Just remember a bigger blanket may need more than 150 yards of your main colors

### What skill level do I need for this pattern?

A confident beginner can finish this lovey comfortably. If you can single crochet, increase, work in continuous rounds with a stitch marker, and do a basic color change, you have every skill required — the 4 assembly steps are simple sewing with a tapestry needle
