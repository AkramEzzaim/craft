This textured Starburst Granny Square is worked over six rounds, beginning with a small circular center and building outward into raised popcorn stitches, a five-point star, and finally a neat square border.

This pattern is best suited to intermediate crocheters or confident beginners who are comfortable with double crochet, treble crochet, joining new yarn, working in chain spaces, and making popcorn stitches. The finished motif works well for blankets, cushions, bags, and other projects where you want more texture than a traditional flat granny square.

## About This Pattern
The Starburst Granny Square is constructed from the center outward over six rounds. Round 1 creates ten evenly spaced double-crochet spokes, while Round 2 places a raised popcorn stitch into each of the ten chain spaces.

Round 3 uses alternating spaces to transform the ten-part circular structure into a five-point star. Taller stitches create the points, while single crochets form the lower valleys between them. Round 4 then fills around the star to produce a more rounded foundation.

Rounds 5 and 6 transform that rounded shape into a traditional square. Different stitch heights are used to build the four corners before a final single-crochet border defines the outer edge.

The pattern practices several useful crochet techniques, including popcorn stitches, picots, treble crochet, back-loop-only work, joining new colors, shaping with different stitch heights, and converting a circular motif into a square. The finished motifs can be joined for blankets, pillow covers, tote bags, decorative panels, or other granny-square projects.

## Instructions
### Round 1 — Create the Center Wheel
**Pattern:** Make a magic loop.

Ch 3, counting as the first dc. Ch 1.

Work **[dc, ch 1] 9 times** into the magic loop.

Pull the center closed firmly.

Sl st to the top of the beginning ch 3. Fasten off.

**Step-by-step:** The beginning ch 3 counts as your first double crochet. After making it, chain 1 to create the first space between the center stitches.

Continue placing one double crochet followed by one chain into the magic loop until you have ten evenly spaced spokes in total. The final chain-1 space is just as important as the others because each of the ten spaces will be used in Round 2.

Pull the magic-loop tail firmly so the center closes without leaving a large hole. Join to the top of the beginning ch 3 and fasten off.

**Stitch count:** 10 dc and 10 ch-1 spaces.

### Round 2 — Make the Popcorn Burst
**Pattern:** Join new yarn in any ch-1 space.

Ch 3, counting as the first dc, then work 3 dc into the same ch-sp to complete the first 4-dc popcorn.

Ch 2.

Work **[4-dc-pc in next ch-sp, ch 2] 9 times**.

Sl st to the top of the first popcorn. Fasten off.

**Step-by-step:** Each chain space from Round 1 receives one popcorn stitch.

For the first popcorn, the beginning ch 3 acts as the first double crochet. Add three more double crochets into the same space. To close the popcorn, remove the active loop from your hook, insert the hook through the top of the beginning ch 3 from front to back, pick up the dropped loop, and pull it through.

For the remaining popcorns, work four double crochets into one chain space. Remove the active loop, insert your hook through the top of the first double crochet from front to back, pick up the dropped loop, and pull it through.

Chain 2 between every popcorn. When complete, you should have ten raised popcorn stitches arranged evenly around the center.

**Stitch count:** 10 popcorn stitches with 10 ch-2 spaces.

### Round 3 — Form the Five-Point Star
**Pattern:** Join new yarn in any ch-2 space.

Ch 2, counting as hdc.

In the same ch-sp work:

**2 dc, tr, ch-3 pic, tr, 2 dc, hdc**

Ch 1.

Sc in the next ch-sp.

Ch 1.

Work:

**[hdc, 2 dc, tr, ch-3 pic, tr, 2 dc, hdc in next ch-sp, ch 1, sc in next ch-sp, ch 1] 4 times**

Sl st to the top of the beginning ch 2. Fasten off.

### Round 4 — uses alternating chain spaces to create five high points and five low valleys.
**Pattern:** In the first chain space, build upward from a half double crochet to double crochets and then a treble crochet. Make the ch-3 picot by chaining 3, then inserting the hook into the third loop of the first chain and completing a slip stitch. Continue in the same chain space with another treble crochet, two double crochets, and one half double crochet.

Chain 1, then place a single crochet into the next chain space. This shorter stitch pulls the fabric downward and creates the valley between two star points.

Continue alternating a complete star-point group with a single-crochet valley. When the round is complete, you should have five clearly defined star points.

### Round 5 — Round Out the Star
**Pattern:** Work this entire round in the **back loops only**.

Join new yarn in the back loop of the second chain of a picot.

Ch 1.

Sc in the next BL, hdc, dc, tr, tr dec, tr, tr dec, tr, dc, hdc, sc.

Work:

**[sl st in the next BL, sc in next BL, hdc, dc, tr, tr dec, tr, tr dec, tr, dc, hdc, sc] 4 times**

Sl st to the first ch 1.

Work only through the back loops. This leaves a visible ridge around the star and helps preserve the dimensional appearance of the previous round.

Begin in the back loop of the second chain of one picot. The stitch sequence gradually increases in height from single crochet to half double crochet, double crochet, and treble crochet, then decreases again.

### Round 6 — Turn the Motif Into a Square
**Pattern:** Ch 2, counting as hdc.

Hdc in the next st.

Sc in the next 4 sts.

Work 2 hdc, 2 dc, tr.

In the corner position work:

**tr, ch 2, tr**

Then work:

**tr, 2 dc**

Continue with:

**[2 hdc, 4 sc, 2 hdc, 2 dc, tr, tr, ch 2, tr in corner, tr, 2 dc] 3 times**

Sl st to the top of the beginning ch 2.

### Round 7 — changes the rounded motif into a four-sided square.
**Pattern:** The shorter single and half double crochets sit along the flatter parts of each side. Double and treble crochets add height as you move toward each corner.

Each corner contains **tr, ch 2, tr**. The ch-2 space becomes the corner opening and provides enough room for the fabric to change direction without pulling tightly.

Continue the side-and-corner sequence around the motif. At the end of the round, you should be able to identify four sides and four ch-2 corner spaces.

### Round 8 — Add the Final Single Crochet Border
**Pattern:** Ch 1, then sc in the same st.

Sc in the next 10 sts.

In the corner space work:

**sc, ch 2, sc**

Sc in the next 5 sts.

Work:

**[11 sc, sc, ch 2, sc in corner space, 5 sc] 3 times**

Sl st to the first sc.

Fasten off.

**Step-by-step:** The final round creates a simple single-crochet border that defines the shape and gives the square a cleaner outer edge.

Work the indicated single crochets across each side. At every ch-2 corner space, work **sc, ch 2, sc** into the same corner space. These extra stitches and chains allow each corner to remain clearly defined.

Continue around all four sides, join to the first single crochet with a slip stitch, and fasten off.

## Finishing
Fasten off the final yarn and weave all loose ends securely into the wrong side of the square.

Because several rounds use separate colors and are fastened off individually, secure each yarn tail carefully without pulling the raised popcorn stitches or star points out of shape.

Gently block the finished square to straighten the sides and corners. The dimensional center may pull the fabric slightly inward, so blocking can help the motif lie more evenly while preserving the raised popcorn texture.

## Important Notes
- **Count the Round 1 spaces carefully.** — You need 10 ch-1 spaces around the center. Each space receives one popcorn in Round 2, so an incorrect count in the first round will affect the rest of the motif.
- **Keep the popcorn stitches facing forward.** — When closing a popcorn, insert the hook through the first dc from front to back before pulling the dropped loop through. This helps the popcorn project toward the right side of the work.

## Variations
### Four-Color Flower Effect
Use one color for the small center, a contrasting color for the popcorn round, another color for the star round, and a fourth color for the square border.

This color arrangement emphasizes both the flower-like center and the star shape.

### Single-Color Textured Star
Work the star section in one color so the design depends mainly on texture rather than contrasting color changes.

The raised popcorn stitches will still create a noticeable three-dimensional burst.

### Larger Granny Square
After completing Round 6, you can add additional granny-style border rounds to enlarge the motif.

Keep the four existing corner spaces aligned and distribute additional stitches evenly along each side so the square continues to lie flat.

### Different Yarn Weights
The motif can be worked in finer or heavier yarn with a hook appropriate for the chosen yarn.

A lighter yarn creates a smaller and more delicate square, while heavier yarn produces a larger and more substantial motif. Make one sample before beginning a project that requires several matching squares.

## Common Mistakes
### Making the Wrong Number of Center Spaces
Round 2 will not produce exactly ten evenly spaced popcorn stitches, and the five-point star in Round 3 may not align correctly.

**How to fix it:** Before fastening off Round 1, count the ch-1 spaces carefully. You should have exactly 10 chain spaces around the center.

### Closing the Popcorn in the Wrong Direction
The popcorn may project toward the back of the square or look flatter than the surrounding popcorn stitches.

**How to fix it:** After working the four dc of the popcorn, remove the active loop. Insert the hook from front to back through the top of the first dc, pick up the dropped loop, and pull it through.

For the first popcorn of Round 2, remember that the beginning ch 3 counts as the first dc, so only three additional dc are worked into that space.

### Making the Ch-2 Spaces Too Loose
The center may begin to ruffle or the popcorn stitches may appear too widely separated.

**How to fix it:** Keep the two chains between popcorn stitches even and controlled. Avoid stretching the active loop while moving from one popcorn to the next.

### Missing a Star Point in Round 3
The motif becomes asymmetrical instead of forming five evenly spaced points.

**How to fix it:** Round 3 alternates between one complete star-point group and one single-crochet valley. Count the completed star points before fastening off. You should have five.

### Misplacing the Round 5 Corners
The motif may become uneven, twisted, or rectangular instead of forming a balanced square.

**How to fix it:** Check for four evenly distributed **tr, ch 2, tr** corner groups before completing the round.

## FAQ
### Is this Starburst Granny Square suitable for beginners?
The pattern is best classified as intermediate because it includes popcorn stitches, picots, treble crochet, back-loop-only work, decreases, and shaped rounds.

A confident beginner who is already comfortable with the basic stitches and has practiced popcorn stitches may still be able to complete it successfully.

### Why does Round 2 have ten popcorn stitches if the star has five points?
The ten popcorn stitches create the textured circular burst.

In Round 3, alternating chain spaces are treated differently. One space forms a raised star point, while the next becomes a lower valley. Repeating this sequence converts the ten-part circle into a five-point star.

### Can I use worsted-weight yarn instead of DK yarn?
Yes. The construction can be worked with a different yarn weight, but the finished dimensions will change.

Choose a hook appropriate for your yarn and make a test square before beginning a larger project that depends on consistent motif sizing.

### Why is my popcorn stitch facing backward?
The popcorn may face the wrong direction if it is closed from the back of the work.

Insert the hook from front to back through the first dc of the popcorn, pick up the dropped loop, and pull it through. This encourages the popcorn to project toward the right side.

### Why is my square curling or ruffling?
Uneven tension, loose chain spaces, yarn substitutions, or incorrect stitch placement can affect how flat the square lies.

First confirm that the round sequences have been followed correctly. If the construction is correct, gently blocking the finished square can help straighten the sides and corners.

### Can I make a blanket from these squares?
Yes. The final square shape makes the motif suitable for joining into blankets and other multi-square projects.

For consistent joining, use the same yarn and hook for every square and block the completed motifs to matching dimensions before assembling them.
